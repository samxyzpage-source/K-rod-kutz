The student service economy, finally with software

Every school is full of businesses.
None of them have tools.

Student barbers, lash techs, tutors, sneaker cleaners and photographers run real money through Instagram DMs and Cash App. Hallway gives every campus seller a booking page, a schedule, SMS reminders, and a reputation — verified, one school at a time.

How it works The math

Bookings live in DMs

Double-bookings and no-shows are constant. One no-show costs a student barber a meaningful slice of the week's income.

Discovery is word of mouth

A great seller is invisible two lunch tables away. There is no directory of who at your school does what.

Incumbents are locked out

Booksy, Fiverr, and Stripe all require sellers to be 18+ or licensed. The constraint that blocks them is the opening.

How it works

01

A page in five minutes

Answer five questions — what you do, what you charge, three photos — and an AI setup flow writes your page: services, prices, gallery, book button. The kind of page that used to take a freelancer a weekend.

02

A real calendar, with reminders

Open slots, automatic SMS confirmations, day-before reminders, and waitlist pings when a slot frees up. Cutting no-shows is an immediate raise — that's why sellers switch in week one. A DM importer reads your existing booking thread and seeds your calendar in one paste.

03

A school-verified reputation

Join with your school email or a verified invite. Real names, public reviews, reliability scores on both sides. Seniors graduate with a portable record — 300 rated cuts is a resume for barber school or a chair at a real shop.

Costs are a student's costs: free-tier hosting, a domain, and about a cent per text message. One school pays for itself at roughly fifteen Pro subscribers.

Scaled the way campuses scale

Launch only where you can hit density. Seed ten sellers, launch the week before homecoming, prove three numbers — no-show rate, rebooking rate, seller revenue — then hand the playbook to one ambassador at the next school. Software costs nothing to copy; a verified trust graph costs everything to catch up to.
